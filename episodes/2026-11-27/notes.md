Rishabh's income comes from salary, capital gains, other sources, and professional income, including through his Hindu undivided family: about six lakh sixty six thousand rupees a month before tax, about one lakh twenty five thousand rupees a month in tax, and a take home of five lakh forty thousand rupees a month. His responsible spending totals one lakh thirty thousand rupees a month: forty thousand in rent and maintenance, twenty thousand in domestic help, ten thousand in groceries, seven thousand in utilities, ten thousand in loan payments, and thirty five thousand in other responsible spending, of which thirty thousand goes toward elderly care for his grandparents and five thousand covers car maintenance; he also pays for his mother's health insurance. His bonus spending totals about one lakh fifty thousand rupees a month, driven mainly by travel (the exact travel figure is not stated in the video), with dining out at fifteen thousand and gadgets at five thousand, bringing total monthly spending to two lakh eighty thousand rupees. He invests just forty thousand rupees a month — twenty thousand into provident fund and pension accounts and twenty thousand into gold — with no mutual fund SIPs at all, favouring derivatives in volatile seasons and large cash and commodity positions for leveraged trading. His portfolio totals one crore seventy six lakh rupees: one crore seventeen lakh in other assets, sixty lakh in cash reserves, twenty five lakh in gold, eighteen lakh in provident fund and pension balances, ten lakh in fixed deposits, and six lakh in direct equities, with no real estate, and some components overlap because his cash also serves as trading capital, so the stated total is the figure to trust. The lessons are to build a five year expense runway so you can stop working at any time, buy term insurance young so the premium stays low for life, plan family care lines early, and budget travel deliberately when travel is what the money is for.
