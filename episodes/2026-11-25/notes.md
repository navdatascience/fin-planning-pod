Pavit's income is a thirty six lakh rupee base salary plus five lakh sixty thousand in variable pay and joining bonus. His share of responsible spending is eighty four thousand four hundred rupees a month, led by forty six thousand in EMIs (thirty one thousand home loan share plus fifteen thousand education loan), sixteen thousand five hundred in rent and maintenance, six thousand in groceries, and five thousand in child care for his three month old daughter. His bonus spending is twenty four thousand one hundred fifty rupees, including seven thousand five hundred on travel and five thousand on dining out, bringing total monthly spending to one lakh eight thousand five hundred fifty rupees. He invests ninety two thousand rupees a month: seventy five thousand in mutual fund SIPs, twelve thousand in provident fund accounts, and five thousand in a recurring deposit building a five lakh emergency fund. His portfolio totals eighty lakh seventy five thousand rupees — forty lakh in real estate, fifteen lakh in provident fund balances, twelve lakh in mutual funds, seven lakh fifty thousand in equities, and one lakh in gold. The lessons are to invest first and spend the rest, split couple expenses through a joint account while keeping personal spends separate, step SIPs up as income grows (his went from three thousand rupees in 2018 to seventy five thousand today), and defend and protect before growing, with insurance and an emergency fund alongside investing.
