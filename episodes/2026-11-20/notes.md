Ganesh Sharma, a software development engineer in Bangalore from Tumkur, married and recently a father to a daughter, earns Rs 77 lakh a year — Rs 68 lakh base salary, Rs 1 lakh variable pay, and Rs 8 lakh in ESOPs. His take-home is Rs 4,16,366 a month after Rs 1,54,384 a month in tax. He spends Rs 68,600 a month — Rs 45,100 responsible (including Rs 20,000 of life insurance, Rs 6,000 utilities, Rs 5,000 groceries, Rs 5,000 child care, and only Rs 3,500 rent and maintenance) and Rs 23,500 bonus — and invests Rs 2,28,000 a month: Rs 1,08,000 in SIPs, Rs 1,00,000 in direct equities bought on intrinsic value, and Rs 20,000 in a post-office RD building his emergency fund. He has made Rs 80 lakh of one-off big spends, including Rs 50 lakh on house construction for his parents. His portfolio totals Rs 1,00,48,573, including Rs 55,24,758 in equities (Indian and US), Rs 14 lakh in gold, Rs 12 lakh in FDs, and Rs 12,61,000 in mutual funds, with no real estate in his own name and Rs 6 lakh in cash reserves. Lessons: buy fundamentally sound stocks when fear makes them cheap, keep an RD and emergency safety net separate from market investments, plan early for the roughly Rs 80 lakh setup phase of a house, marriage, and education, and remember that money buys freedom and time once the foundation is built.
