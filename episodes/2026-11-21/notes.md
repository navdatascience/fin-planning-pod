Damas Varsi, a 33-year-old senior manager in software engineering in Gurgaon, unmarried with no dependants, earns Rs 93 lakh a year — Rs 60 lakh base salary, Rs 8 lakh rental income from a Goa commercial property leased for 15 years, and Rs 25 lakh of other income (Rs 20 lakh variable pay and about Rs 5 lakh from content creation). His take-home is Rs 4,93,635 a month. He spends Rs 2,52,648 a month — Rs 1,61,999 responsible (including Rs 70,000 of loan repayments, Rs 40,000 sponsoring the education of three children, and Rs 30,000 rent and maintenance) and Rs 90,649 bonus, with travel the largest part — and invests Rs 2,36,000 a month: Rs 1,00,000 into international markets around the AI theme, Rs 50,000 in Indian mutual fund SIPs, Rs 50,000 in gold, and Rs 36,000 in EPF, leaving a monthly surplus of just Rs 4,987. He has made Rs 70 lakh of one-off big spends, including about Rs 20 lakh on house construction and about Rs 50 lakh as plot down payments. His net worth totals Rs 9.22 crore, including Rs 5 crore in real estate, Rs 2.9 crore in equities (Rs 1.2 crore in India and Rs 1.7 crore international), Rs 60 lakh in gold, Rs 35 lakh in EPF, a Rs 17 lakh emergency fund split across four FDs, and Rs 20 lakh in cash reserves. Lessons: upskilling is the highest-return investment (he went from Rs 25,000 a month to Rs 93 lakh a year through seven job switches in ten years), diversify across asset classes and geographies, split an emergency fund into smaller FDs so only the needed one is broken, and spend on needs and family security over luxury wants.
